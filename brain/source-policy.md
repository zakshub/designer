# Source and Evidence Policy

## Source hierarchy

Tier 1
Standards bodies, platform owners, official design systems, original research and primary material.

Tier 2
Established practitioners, studios, conference talks, books, interviews and documented case studies.

Tier 3
High quality industry publications, award archives and specialist educational resources.

Tier 4
Community discussion and social posts. Useful for signals and emerging practice, never sufficient alone for canonical rules.

## Evidence record

Every retained insight should store:

source title
source URL
author or organization
retrieval date
publication date when available
domain tags
source tier
claim or principle
context
confidence
contradictions
related experts
related skills

## Copyright boundary

Store distilled principles, metadata, brief quotations when necessary and links back to originals. Do not mirror complete copyrighted articles, books, courses or proprietary design files.

## Promotion rule

A raw observation becomes canonical only after one of these conditions is met:

1. It is an explicit authoritative standard
2. It is supported by multiple credible independent sources
3. It is a practitioner specific principle and remains clearly attributed
4. It has been validated through documented usability or production evidence

## Freshness

Platform guidance, accessibility requirements, tooling practices and AI interface patterns require periodic freshness checks. Historical design theory can remain stable but should still retain source dates.
