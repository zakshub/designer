# Cross-Category Case-Study Bank

Purpose: widen the solution space and study mechanisms rather than copy surface style.

Each case records why a visual decision was useful in its context. Do not generalize the output into a category recipe.

## Finance / Fintech

### PayPal — Pentagram
Source: https://www.pentagram.com/work/paypal
Context: global financial technology brand seeking simplicity, optimism and trust.
Mechanism to study:
- deliberately reduced reliance on the blue strongly associated with fintech;
- black/white became the foundation while blue remained accent/energy;
- brand motion was derived from real payment gestures such as taps, flips and swipes;
- photography carries more brand personality when the core UI palette is restrained.
Lesson: category familiarity can be retained without letting the category's dominant hue own the entire interface.

### Payz — Pentagram
Source: https://www.pentagram.com/work/payz
Context: challenger payment service needing trust, clarity and human tone in a crowded market.
Mechanism to study:
- black/white/yellow plus a kraft neutral to counter over-technical finance language;
- oversized punctuation became a recognizable device;
- informal, jargon-free voice combined with bold typography.
Lesson: a fintech brand can signal confidence through composition, type and a distinctive accent rather than default institutional blue.

### Wirex — Pentagram
Source: https://www.pentagram.com/work/wirex
Context: digital money platform bridging traditional and crypto currencies for everyday use.
Mechanism to study:
- dark/light green with pastels and greys;
- playful illustration and icon system softened the technical category;
- visual language was carried consistently across product and communications.
Lesson: everyday accessibility can justify a friendlier finance palette and illustration layer.

### Wings — Pentagram
Source: https://www.pentagram.com/work/wings
Context: premium financial offer for affluent customers.
Mechanism to study:
- dark turquoise, steel and ivory selected to stand apart in finance;
- sophisticated lifestyle photography reinforced premium positioning.
Lesson: premium finance need not automatically become black/gold; category differentiation can come from less saturated, unusual hue families plus art direction.

### Tala — Pentagram / Paula Scher
Source: https://www.pentagram.com/work/tala
Context: financial services for underbanked customers across emerging markets.
Mechanism to study:
- approachable, diverse shapes inspired by local architecture;
- broad color/pattern language supports inclusion and recognizability;
- trust is built through accessibility and cultural resonance rather than institutional mimicry.
Lesson: geographic and audience context can be more important than conventional fintech aesthetics.

### Droit — Pentagram / Natasha Jen
Source: https://www.pentagram.com/work/droit
Context: regulatory fintech platform operating in complex, changing legal systems.
Mechanism to study:
- visual concept based on transformation between volatile complexity and safe resolution;
- dual/custom type language expressed change and completion;
- blue/black palette supported, rather than created, the strategic concept.
Lesson: concept first, palette second.

## Health / Wellness

### Abridge — Pentagram / Natasha Jen
Source: https://www.pentagram.com/work/abridge
Context: AI clinical conversation platform for physicians and patients.
Mechanism to study:
- positioning around “Taken care of” balanced competence with humanity;
- bridge/architecture metaphor generated logo, type, motion and visual system;
- generative typography conveyed AI without falling into generic AI-orb aesthetics.
Lesson: health + AI can be expressed through a coherent conceptual system instead of combining healthcare blue with AI gradients.

### Phenology — Pentagram
Source: https://www.pentagram.com/work/phenology
Context: menopause care positioned between medicine and luxury self-care.
Mechanism to study:
- black/white/beige primary palette plus vivid secondary/neon accents;
- serif + sans pairing reflected scientific expertise and self-care sophistication;
- product-specific colors were named from natural ingredients.
Lesson: a health/wellness brand can intentionally reject delicate category colors when positioning demands confidence and visibility.

### Kids Eat in Color — Pentagram
Source: https://www.pentagram.com/work/kids-eat-in-color
Context: evidence-based nutrition/feeding education for parents and children.
Mechanism to study:
- palette drawn from food itself;
- visual identity aimed for knowledgeable, trustworthy and non-judgmental;
- design explicitly avoided worn clichés such as rainbows, carrots, crayons and utensils.
Lesson: category symbols should be interrogated, not automatically used.

## Food & Drink

### Snap Kitchen — Pentagram
Source: https://www.pentagram.com/work/snap-kitchen
Context: healthy prepared meals to go.
Mechanism to study:
- simple graphic ingredients created a modular system;
- color-coding was functional, helping organize menu/product information.
Lesson: color can perform information architecture, not merely appetite/brand mood.

### Cocorico — Pentagram
Source: https://www.pentagram.com/work/cocorico/story
Context: natural poultry brand emphasizing health, ethical standards and culinary culture.
Mechanism to study:
- blue led the identity rather than expected red/orange/green food cues;
- broader spectrum reflected culinary richness;
- high-contrast food photography and custom type created appetite and character.
Lesson: photography, material and narrative can carry “food” even when the lead brand hue is unconventional.

### Blank Street — Wolff Olins
Source: https://www.wolffolins.com/work/blank-street
Context: coffee brand moving from transaction to a more distinctive lifestyle experience.
Mechanism to study:
- green palette shifts through the day;
- typography deliberately balances regular/everyday and remarkable moments;
- photography takes cues from fashion and vintage editorial rather than coffee-stock imagery.
Lesson: adjacent-category references can create differentiation when rooted in strategy.

### Bite Back — Wolff Olins
Source: https://www.wolffolins.com/work/bite-back
Context: youth health advocacy challenging junk-food marketing.
Mechanism to study:
- intentionally appropriates and subverts junk-food color/typographic tactics;
- visual language is confrontational because the communication strategy requires it.
Lesson: category language can be weaponized or inverted when the brand's role is opposition rather than participation.

## Fashion / Beauty / Luxury

### Milly — Pentagram / Natasha Jen
Source: https://www.pentagram.com/work/milly
Context: fashion label with vibrant garments and patterns.
Mechanism to study:
- black/white brand palette deliberately receded so the clothing's color could dominate;
- geometric type and fluid mark balanced strength and femininity.
Lesson: monochrome was a product-framing strategy, not a default luxury formula.

### Benefit — Wolff Olins
Source: https://www.wolffolins.com/work/benefit
Context: beauty brand needing stronger global recognition and a refreshed playful identity.
Mechanism to study:
- existing pink was strengthened into a distinctive behavior, not replaced;
- graphic language extends brand personality through faces/features and playful movement.
Lesson: an existing category-associated color can still be valuable when it is distinctive, ownable and expressed in a proprietary system.

### Plenaire — Pentagram
Source: https://www.pentagram.com/work/plenaire
Context: ethical skincare for young consumers seeking accessible luxury.
Mechanism to study:
- light violet accent derived from a real scientific stain;
- transparent packaging, sparse black type and small particle motifs supported lightness and science;
- photography carried relaxed natural beauty.
Lesson: color becomes stronger when it has a brand-specific origin story and is supported by material and imagery.

### Fashion for Good — Pentagram
Source: https://www.pentagram.com/work/fashion-for-good
Context: global initiative involving multiple fashion-industry partners.
Mechanism to study:
- type-led system with flexible colorways;
- partner colors can run through the identity without destroying coherence.
Lesson: brand systems can be designed to absorb external palettes when ecosystem flexibility is a core requirement.

## How to use this bank

For a new product:
1. retrieve two or more cases with similar strategic problems, not merely the same industry;
2. extract the mechanism and constraint;
3. add counterexamples from the same category;
4. compare against shipped product UI references;
5. create original directions rather than reproducing any case's visual output.

Practitioner case studies describe design intent and implementation. They are not controlled evidence that a given visual choice caused business results.
