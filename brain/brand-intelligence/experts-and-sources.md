# Experts, Research and Reference Sources

Research pass: 2026-10-02.

This registry is for attributed design reasoning. Inclusion does not imply endorsement, universal correctness, or permission to imitate a person's recognizable style. Preserve context and limitations.

## Evidence-led SMEs

### Lauren I. Labrecque
Domain: color in marketing, brand personality, consumer perception.
Basis: peer-reviewed empirical research.
Key source: Labrecque & Milne, “Exciting red and competent blue: the importance of color in marketing,” Journal of the Academy of Marketing Science (2012).
Locator: https://doi.org/10.1007/s11747-010-0245-y
Use for: evidence that hue, saturation and value can shape brand perception.
Limit: do not convert findings into universal category-color rules.

### George R. Milne
Domain: marketing, consumer behavior, color and brand perception.
Basis: co-author of the Labrecque & Milne empirical work.
Locator: https://doi.org/10.1007/s11747-010-0245-y
Use for: same evidence family as above; counts as the same origin, not an independent corroborating source.

### Andrew J. Elliot
Domain: color psychology and context effects.
Basis: Annual Review of Psychology review with Markus A. Maier.
Locator: https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-psych-010213-115035
Use for: understanding that color meaning can affect cognition/behavior while remaining context-dependent.
Limit: authors explicitly note boundary-condition and generalizability limits.

### Markus A. Maier
Domain: color psychology, affect and contextual interpretation.
Basis: co-author of the Annual Review synthesis.
Locator: https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-psych-010213-115035
Use for: methodological caution around simplistic color psychology.

### Jenni Romaniuk
Domain: distinctive brand assets, brand memory and identification.
Basis: Ehrenberg-Bass Institute research and publications.
Reference: https://marketingscience.info/news-and-insights/brands-of-distinction
Use for: evaluating whether color, font, shapes and other elements are actually distinctive assets rather than merely attractive brand elements.
Core concept: distinctiveness depends on brand linkage/uniqueness and prevalence in memory.

### Jiaqi (Flora) Song, Fei (Katie) Xu, Yuwei Jiang
Domain: logo colorfulness and consumer judgment.
Basis: peer-reviewed Psychology & Marketing experiments.
Locator: https://doi.org/10.1002/mar.21674
Use for: evidence that colorfulness itself can influence perceived product variety and that brand positioning moderates effects.
Limit: logo-level experimental evidence does not directly prescribe full product UI palettes.

## Source strength guidance

- Peer-reviewed empirical/review research: strong for narrow claims within study limits.
- Platform/design-system guidance: strong for implementation/accessibility patterns within that ecosystem; contextual outside it.
- Named agency case study: practitioner evidence about goals, mechanisms and delivered decisions; not causal proof.
- Critical publication: useful critique and historical/contextual comparison; opinion remains attributed.
- Shipped-product library: observational reference; common usage is not validation.

No source in this file is preapproved as universal guidance. Claims promoted into Sentinel's active knowledge graph still require the repository evidence/review process.
