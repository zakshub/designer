# Category Visual-Language Priors

These are investigation prompts, not prescriptions. A category prior describes what to inspect before designing; it does not dictate a palette.

## Finance / Fintech

User needs often include trust, clarity, security, speed, control, numerical legibility and confidence under uncertainty.

Inspect:
- institutional vs challenger positioning;
- consumer vs professional sophistication;
- regulated vs informal context;
- transactional vs analytical use;
- calm reassurance vs energetic growth;
- competitor saturation of blue/navy/green;
- status semantics in the target culture;
- tabular numerals, charts and dense data.

Common cliché risk:
- navy/royal blue as automatic trust signal;
- crypto-neon aesthetics regardless of product;
- dark trading-terminal treatment for simple consumer finance;
- green used indiscriminately for all positive brand expression.

Useful counterexamples to study:
- PayPal's move toward black/white with blue as accent;
- Payz's black/white/yellow plus kraft tone;
- Wings' dark turquoise, steel and ivory premium positioning;
- Tala's locally informed shapes and broad color system for underbanked markets;
- Wirex's green plus pastels for an everyday digital-money tone.

## Health / Clinical / Wellness

User needs can include safety, clarity, dignity, calm, evidence, empathy, privacy, urgency and accessibility.

Inspect:
- clinical workflow vs consumer wellness;
- high-stakes diagnostic/medical context vs lifestyle support;
- professional clinician audience vs patient audience;
- anxiety level of the task;
- accessibility and age-related vision needs;
- use of sterile white/blue/green conventions;
- whether warmth or technical precision should dominate.

Common cliché risk:
- universal medical blue/teal;
- generic leaf/heart/cross iconography;
- excessive pastel softness that reduces authority;
- pure clinical minimalism that removes humanity.

Useful counterexamples to study:
- Abridge: precision + care, with a generative architectural/typographic system instead of generic AI/health clichés;
- Phenology: black/white/beige plus neon secondary accents to balance medical science and luxury self-care;
- Kids Eat in Color: palette inspired by food while explicitly avoiding obvious rainbow/carrot/crayon/utensil clichés.

## Food & Drink

User needs vary sharply: appetite, freshness, speed, craft, provenance, indulgence, health, affordability, familiarity or novelty.

Inspect:
- restaurant vs packaged goods vs delivery vs nutrition education;
- premium craft vs mass convenience;
- ingredient provenance;
- photography importance;
- packaging shelf competition;
- cultural food symbolism;
- appetite cues from real ingredients rather than generic color psychology.

Common cliché risk:
- automatic red/orange for appetite;
- leaf green for every healthy product;
- rustic kraft for every natural product;
- over-styled food photography disconnected from the actual offer.

Useful counterexamples to study:
- Kids Eat in Color: food-derived palette tied to educational/health mission;
- Snap Kitchen: color-coded information system tied to menu/product organization;
- Cocorico: blue-led poultry identity with broader culinary color range, showing food brands need not default to warm hues;
- Blank Street: a green palette that changes through the day, paired with fashion/editorial art direction.

## Fashion / Beauty / Luxury

User needs may include desire, identity, taste, status, self-expression, materiality, storytelling and cultural relevance.

Inspect:
- luxury vs accessible luxury vs youth vs performance;
- product color already present in garments/cosmetics;
- photography/art direction dominance;
- editorial vs retail requirements;
- heritage vs contemporary positioning;
- need for neutral framing vs expressive brand color.

Common cliché risk:
- black/white as automatic luxury;
- cream/serif/editorial treatment reused for every premium brand;
- pink as automatic feminine/beauty cue;
- minimalism that erases product personality.

Useful counterexamples to study:
- Milly: black/white chosen specifically to frame vibrant garments, not because fashion must be monochrome;
- Benefit: pink treated as an active brand asset with playful graphic behavior;
- Phenology: medical/wellness category conventions deliberately challenged by a luxury/science duality;
- Plenaire: light violet derived from a scientific stain and used as an accent rather than filling the whole system;
- Fashion for Good: flexible colorways that adapt to partner contexts while preserving a type-led system.

## Enterprise / B2B / Developer Tools

User needs often include speed, density, precision, hierarchy, reliability, collaboration and repeated long-duration use.

Inspect:
- novice vs expert operator;
- task frequency;
- information density;
- dark-theme demand in coding/operations contexts;
- chart/table requirements;
- semantic state load;
- brand expression that does not reduce work efficiency.

Common cliché risk:
- generic dark mode + purple/blue gradient for anything technical;
- excessive cards and dashboards;
- brand color used on every interactive element;
- fashionable low-contrast interfaces that fail prolonged use.

Reference systems:
- IBM Carbon / IBM Design Language;
- Adobe Spectrum;
- Atlassian Design System;
- real shipped examples via Mobbin, Refero and SaaSFrame.

## Government / Civic / Public Service

User needs include trust, universality, clarity, accessibility, low learning cost and resilience across devices and demographics.

Inspect:
- legal/official requirements;
- accessibility first;
- language/localization;
- low-bandwidth and older-device conditions;
- high consequence forms/tasks;
- institutional consistency vs agency-specific identity.

Common cliché risk:
- ornamental branding that competes with essential information;
- inaccessible patriotic color combinations;
- excessive density without hierarchy.

Reference:
- U.S. Web Design System role-based theme and state tokens, used as a system example rather than an aesthetic target.

## Kids / Education

User needs vary by age: clarity, motivation, safety, play, progress, comprehension, caregiver trust and educational seriousness.

Inspect:
- exact age range;
- child vs parent vs teacher as decision-maker;
- content density;
- motor/cognitive ability;
- accessibility;
- whether playfulness supports or distracts from learning.

Common cliché risk:
- rainbow by default;
- childish illustration for older learners;
- saturated color everywhere;
- confusing playfulness with poor hierarchy.

## Entertainment / Gaming / Media

User needs can include immersion, discovery, excitement, collection, identity, fandom and extended viewing.

Inspect:
- content should dominate vs chrome should dominate;
- dark environments for cinematic content;
- genre conventions;
- motion/audio role;
- high-saturation imagery interaction with UI;
- accessibility during long sessions.

Common cliché risk:
- neon dark UI regardless of genre;
- excessive glow;
- hiding navigation for cinematic effect;
- visual identity competing with artwork/media.

## Rule for all categories

The final direction must be explained from product positioning, user tasks, competitive context and brand assets. Category membership alone is never sufficient justification.
